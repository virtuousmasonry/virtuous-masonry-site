Stone veneer vs. brick veneer: which is right for your home?

Cost, durability, and where each one actually makes sense on a Lehigh Valley home.

The short answer

Brick veneer costs less and makes more sense for covering a full exterior. Stone veneer costs more but delivers a premium look that's often used strategically — an entryway, a fireplace surround, porch columns, or a foundation accent — rather than an entire house. Neither is "better" outright; they solve different problems at different price points.

Durability and appearance

Brick veneer is manufactured, so every piece matches consistently, giving a uniform look. It can chip and shows less texture variation than stone. Stone veneer is cut to mimic natural stone's irregular color and texture, which is exactly what makes it read as higher-end — and it holds color well since it's not painted or dyed in a way that fades evenly across a surface.

Installation and what to watch for locally

Both types need a code-compliant water-resistant barrier behind them and correct flashing to keep moisture out of the wall assembly. In the Lehigh Valley's freeze-thaw climate, the mortar mix used to set either veneer needs to account for repeated freezing — a mismatched or overly rigid mortar mix is one of the most common reasons a veneer installation fails within a few years instead of lasting the 30–50 years it should. This matters more than which veneer type you pick.

Which one should you choose

If you're covering a full exterior on a budget, brick veneer is the more economical, cohesive choice. If you want a premium look concentrated on a specific feature — an entry, a fireplace, an accent wall — stone veneer's higher cost per square foot is manageable because you're covering less area. Key takeaways

  • Brick veneer runs $10–$22/sq ft; stone veneer runs $13–$30+/sq ft depending on material.
  • Brick veneer suits full-exterior coverage; stone veneer is often used strategically on accent areas.
  • Correct mortar mix and flashing matter more than veneer type for surviving freeze-thaw winters.

FAQ

Can I mix brick and stone veneer on the same home?

Yes, this is common — brick veneer for the main exterior with stone veneer as an accent on an entryway or foundation line is a popular combination.

Does stone veneer increase home value more than brick?

Both can add curb appeal and value; stone veneer's premium look often has a stronger visual impact per square foot when used as an accent.

How long does veneer installation take?

Depends on area covered, but most residential accent installations take a few days to a week; full-exterior jobs take longer.
