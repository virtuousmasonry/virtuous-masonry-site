Types of retaining wall materials: pros, cons, and costs

Concrete block, natural stone, brick, and timber compared for a Lehigh Valley yard.

Most Lehigh Valley retaining walls are built from one of four materials: segmental concrete block, natural stone, brick, or timber. Each has a different balance of cost, durability, and appearance — here's how they actually compare once a Pennsylvania winter is part of the equation.

Segmental concrete block

Engineered concrete block is the most common retaining wall material locally, and for good reason: it's faster to build than full-bed stone, comes in a wide range of colors and textures, and offers engineered strength for taller walls. The main downside is that joints stay visible and colors can fade somewhat over time. For most residential walls, this is the best balance of cost and durability — see current pricing in our retaining wall cost guide.

Natural stone

Stone retaining walls have a classic, high-end look and are extremely durable once built. The tradeoff is cost — both materials and the labor to fit irregular stones correctly run higher than block — and the weight of the stone can complicate installation on difficult sites.

Brick

Brick retaining walls are strong and match traditional architecture well, which suits a lot of the older housing stock in Allentown and Bethlehem. Installation is labor-intensive and typically costs more than block, and it's less commonly used for taller structural walls than block or stone.

Timber

Wood or timber walls are the least expensive option upfront and work fine for small, low walls. In the Lehigh Valley's freeze-thaw climate, though, wood is prone to rot and insect damage over time and needs regular treatment to last — it's a reasonable choice for a short-term or budget project, not for a wall meant to last decades.

What actually matters more than material

Whichever material you choose, the wall's long-term performance depends more on drainage and backfill than on what's visible on the surface. A concrete block wall with proper drainage will outlast a stone wall built without it. Material choice is mostly about look, budget, and how the wall fits your property — not a shortcut around getting the base and drainage right.

Key takeaways

  • Segmental concrete block offers the best balance of cost and durability for most Lehigh Valley walls.
  • Natural stone and brick cost more but offer a different aesthetic, especially on older homes.
  • Timber is budget-friendly short-term but prone to rot in this freeze-thaw climate.
  • Drainage and backfill matter more than material for how long a wall actually lasts.

FAQ

Which retaining wall material lasts longest?

Properly built concrete block, stone, and brick walls can all last 30+ years with adequate drainage — the installation quality matters more than the material choice.

Is timber ever a good choice?

For small, low, budget-conscious projects, yes — but expect a shorter lifespan than masonry options in this climate.
