Signs your chimney needs repointing

What to actually check for, and which signs mean it's time to call before winter makes it worse.

Chimney mortar doesn't fail all at once — it shows warning signs for months or years before a joint actually gives way. Catching those signs early, especially before a Lehigh Valley winter, is what keeps a small repointing job from turning into a full rebuild.

Mortar you can scrape out with a fingernail

Sound mortar is hard and doesn't crumble under light pressure. If you can dig a small chunk out of a joint with a fingernail or a screwdriver tip, that mortar has already lost its structural integrity, even if the chimney still looks fine from the ground.

Mortar debris on the roof or in the gutter

Small chunks or grit collecting near the base of the chimney or in a nearby gutter after wind or rain is a sign mortar is actively breaking down and shedding, not just aging cosmetically.

White, chalky residue on the brick (efflorescence)

That white staining is mineral deposit left behind as water moves through masonry and evaporates. It means moisture is getting into the chimney structure somewhere — often through failed mortar joints — and is worth investigating even if the brick itself still looks intact.

Visible gaps or daylight through the joints

If you can see daylight through a chimney joint from inside an unfinished attic, or a joint has a visible gap you can fit a coin into, that's well past early-stage wear and needs attention before the next freeze-thaw cycle widens it further.

Loose or shifting bricks

Individual bricks that wiggle or have visibly shifted out of alignment mean the mortar holding them is no longer doing its job structurally, not just cosmetically.

Why timing matters in this climate

The Lehigh Valley goes through months of freeze-thaw cycles every winter. Water that gets into a small crack freezes, expands, and widens that crack a little more each cycle. A joint that needs a modest spot repair in October can need a much larger repair — or a full rebuild — by spring if it's left through the winter. Catching these signs in fall, before the freeze-thaw season starts, is the cheapest time to act.

Key takeaways

  • Mortar you can scrape out with a fingernail has already lost its structural integrity.
  • Efflorescence (white chalky residue) signals moisture moving through the masonry.
  • Visible gaps or loose bricks mean the damage has progressed past early-stage wear.
  • Fall, before freeze-thaw season starts, is the cheapest time to catch and fix these signs.
