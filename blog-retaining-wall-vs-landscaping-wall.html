Retaining wall vs. landscaping wall: what's the difference?

They can look almost identical. Only one of them is holding your yard in place.

The core difference

A retaining wall is a structural element engineered to hold back soil on a slope that wouldn't otherwise stay put — it manages real pressure and prevents erosion. A landscaping wall (sometimes called a garden wall) is decorative: it defines a flower bed, borders a walkway, or edges a patio, without holding back a meaningful amount of soil or water pressure.

Height is the quickest tell

Landscaping walls are typically under about 2 feet and are built strong enough to hold back a shallow bed of soil — 1.5 to 2 feet at most. Retaining walls are generally 3 feet or taller and use reinforcement like geogrid and engineered backfill that a landscaping wall simply doesn't need. If a wall is taller than about 2–3 feet and actually separating two different ground levels, it's functioning as a retaining wall whether it was built like one or not.

Why building a landscaping wall where you need a retaining wall is a real problem

This mix-up happens more often than you'd think, usually on a budget project: a homeowner or a landscaper builds a low decorative wall to hold back what turns out to be a genuine slope, without the reinforcement or drainage a retaining wall needs. It looks fine the first season. Then the ground behind it saturates, freezes, and the wall leans, bulges, or fails outright — and fixing it means tearing out the failed wall and rebuilding it correctly, which costs more than building it right the first time would have.

How to tell which one you actually need

Ask: is this wall separating two meaningfully different ground levels, or holding back a slope that would otherwise erode? If yes, it needs to be engineered as a retaining wall — reinforcement, proper backfill, and drainage — regardless of how short it looks. If it's purely decorative, dividing flat ground into garden beds or walkway borders, a simpler landscaping wall is the right (and more affordable) choice.

Key takeaways

  • Retaining walls are structural and hold back soil pressure; landscaping walls are decorative.
  • Height is the quickest indicator — over roughly 2–3 feet holding back a real slope means retaining wall territory.
  • Building a decorative wall where a retaining wall is needed is a common, costly mistake in this climate.
  • When in doubt, have it assessed — underbuilding a retaining wall usually costs more to fix later than to build correctly upfront.

FAQ

Can a landscaping wall be upgraded to a retaining wall later?

Usually not without rebuilding it — the reinforcement and drainage need to be part of the original construction, not added afterward.

How much more does a retaining wall cost than a landscaping wall?

Significantly more per square foot, due to the reinforcement and drainage involved — see our retaining wall cost guide for current pricing.

What materials work for landscaping walls?

The same materials used for retaining walls — concrete block, stone, brick — work fine for landscaping walls too, just without the engineering requirements. See our materials comparison for the tradeoffs.
